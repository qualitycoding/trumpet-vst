Implementable scheme (derived from the exact implicit solution of eq. (2), Poisson/Earnshaw simple wave, valid before shock formation): output at the end of an equivalent cylinder of length Leq: p_out(t) = p_in(t - Leq/c0 + beta Leq p_in / (rho0 c0^3)) evaluated implicitly, i.e. each input sample s_k = p_in(t_k) arrives at time t_k + Leq/c0 - beta Leq s_k/(rho0 c0^3): positive pressure arrives early. Real-time realisation = amplitude-dependent fractional delay / time warping: compute arrival times for incoming samples, resample onto the uniform output grid (monotonic as long as Leq < x_s; when arrival times would cross, a shock has formed - apply weak-shock rule or simply limit dp/dt/oversample and low-pass). Delta delay for p = 10 kPa, Leq = 0.85 m: beta Leq p/(rho c^3) = 1.2*0.85*1e4/(1.2*4.04e7) = 0.21 ms (~9 samples at 44.1 kHz). Add wall losses as a linear low-pass/fractional (sqrt(jw)) filter in series, split into a few segments (operator splitting as in Berjamin et al. 2017). Berjamin uses a TVD finite-volume scheme + diffusive approximation of the fractional integral (too slow for real time: 18 min per simulated second). Berjamin results: nonlinear propagation raised playing frequency by up to 45 Hz (+157 cents) in transients, and altered thresholds/register behaviour - output-side-only modelling (no feedback) ignores these effects.
